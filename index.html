Why "Third Parent"

I became one before I had a word for it.

My brother was diagnosed with schizophrenia. Nobody handed me a manual. No counselor asked if I was okay. No form had a box for "sibling of someone living with psychosis."

So I built the thing I needed. Not a parent, not a professional — something in between, doing work that rarely gets a name, let alone support.

"It comes with silence, stigma, and a kid still expected to turn in homework on time."

Our Theory of Change

You can't support a caregiver you can't see.

Schools ask dozens of intake questions. Almost none ask this: "Do you help care for a family member with a mental illness or disability?" Our plan starts there.

1

Ask the question

One line, added to intake forms schools already send.

2

Reach out directly

A real person contacts every student who says yes.

3

Train the counselors

Extensions and check-ins, backed by actual training.

4

Connect to support

A warm handoff into groups and resources — not a link.

5

Measure it

Track outcomes so the policy proves itself.

Physical caregiving is visible. Psychiatric caregiving isn't. A student who misses school for a parent's chemotherapy can say so out loud. A student who misses school because their brother is in a psychiatric unit usually can't — not without inviting stigma from a teacher who's never been trained to hold it.

Our plan starts with the identifying question because you can't fix what schools never measure. From there, we track grades before and after accommodations go in place. See Our Plan.

Monthly Newsletter

The Third Parent Letter

Short, opinionated notes on caregiving and psychiatry — mine, not a press release.

One email a month. Unsubscribe anytime.

A broken leg gets a cast and a casserole. A psychiatric diagnosis gets silence. HIPAA protects the patient — as it should — but it also means the person driving to appointments and de-escalating at 2am often has no legal standing to even be told the diagnosis.

Schools have language for a kid caring for a grandparent with dementia. Almost none have language for a kid going home to a parent mid-episode. Until we name it, we can't build policy around it.

"Mental health" today means therapy apps and mindfulness. Important, but a different world from caring for someone through psychosis or an involuntary hold.

I chose "psychiatric" on purpose. I don't want what my family went through diluted into a wellness hashtag. The caregivers I'm trying to reach need something built for their exact situation, not adjacent to it.
